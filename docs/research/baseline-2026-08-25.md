## Metrics split by how much they can be trusted

**This split is the point of the document.** The 0.0.1 → 0.2.0 comparison in
`loop-cost-and-convergence.md` was confounded by time, milestone and issue difficulty, and it will
happen again here if everything is treated as one bucket.

### Direct — a near-mechanical function of engine size. Predict these.

- always-loaded footprint (bytes on disk — not even a measurement, an input)
- engine-load tokens per session
- engine share of file-read volume

### Confounded — real, but move with whatever issues happen to be worked. Observe, do not predict magnitude.

- peak parent context
- parent tokens processed
- subagent runs, gate rounds, journal entries per issue

A confounded metric moving the right way is **weak** evidence. Moving the wrong way is worth
investigating. Neither settles anything on n≈5.

## Predictions, stated in advance

For the incremental change — defer `accepting` plus a `reference` appendix, targeting **~30k
always-loaded**:

| # | metric | baseline | predicted | class |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| P1 | always-loaded footprint | 45,937 | **~30,000** | direct |
| P2 | engine load / session, full iteration | 53,693 | **~38,000** | direct |
| P3 | engine load / session, plan-gate stop | 53,693 | **~30,000** | direct |
| P4 | engine share of file-read volume | 59% | **~45%** | direct |
| P5 | peak parent context | 312k–534k | **down ~15–20k**, same order | confounded |
| P6 | **subagent-runs per issue** | median 6 | **unchanged** | confounded |
| P7 | **gate-rounds per issue** | median 5 | **unchanged** | confounded |
| **P2c** | **engine share of avg context** (`resident/processed`) | **median 20.8%** (n=7 adm.) | **~14%** | direct |
| **P9** | **compaction count per session** | 0–3 | **down** | confounded |

**P6 and P7 are the important ones, and they are predictions of *no change*.** Sharding moves where
instructions are stored; it touches nothing about convergence. **If gate rounds fall after sharding,
the most likely explanation is confounding, not success** — and treating it as success is how the
0.0.1/0.2.0 comparison would have misled us. A drop is a prompt to check what issues were worked,
not a result.

**If P1–P4 hold and P5–P7 do not move,** the change did what it was designed to do and nothing else.
That is the outcome to want.

### P2c — added 2026-08-26, revised same day after architect review

P1–P4 measure **ingestion**: engine tokens counted once, when they land. That tracks the lever but
is not a cost proxy. Measured across seven admissible 0.2.0 sessions, the engine is carried for
**~91% of every run** and is **~18% of the bill**. Method and full table: Finding 10 in
`loop-cost-and-convergence.md`.

**P2c is primary; P2 is secondary.** P2 is blind to *when* a unit loads — a run loading all seven
units at turn 5 scores identically to one deferring six of them. But **P2c must be expressed
turn-invariantly** (`resident_turns / processed`), because raw resident-turn tokens scale with turn
count, the most confounded quantity in this corpus. Raw P2c on a hard issue would read *up* after a
successful sharding, rebuilding the exact trap this document's "direct vs confounded" split exists
to avoid.

**P8 has been deleted, not renumbered-out.** Engine-share-of-bill and P2c are one measurement
reported twice (`P8 ≈ P2c/processed`, scaled by the output fraction). #128 must not carry both as
independent acceptance criteria. **P9 (compaction count) replaces it** and is genuinely independent:
compaction is currently the only thing reducing engine residency, and it does so by forcing a
re-read at cache-*write* rates. Sharding's second-order benefit may exceed its first-order one.

**⚠ The P2 figures in the tables below are wrong and are kept only as a record.** Three detection
bugs (heredoc writes counted as reads; working-tree reads counted as loads; spill-file recovery
reads invisible) plus a `chars/4` estimate. Session `d9933e33` was recorded here as **9 reads /
53,693 tokens**; it is **8 reads / 62,014**. **Do not compare any post-v0.2.1 measurement against
the tables below** — compare against Finding 10's table.

**Revised expectation for the sharding epic: ~4–5% of a run's bill, not ~7%.** The 46k→30k figure is
P1, the file on disk; applying it proportionally to residency conflates deletion with deferral. In a
full iteration the deferred units still load and only the arrival centroid moves. ~7% is the
ceiling, reached where deferred units are never loaded (plan-gate stops, reference appendix). **Set
the epic's target at the lower figure or it will miss while working correctly.**

**Admissibility is a precondition of measurement.** A session counts only if measured ingestion
clears one copy of the engine (~50.7k tokens). Below the floor it is an *unmeasured* run, not a
cheap one — excluded loudly, never averaged in. This replaces the "pair P2 with a completeness
check" proposal, which was an enumeration; this is default-deny.

**Prefer the vote repo, but not exclusively.** Vote never edits the engine, so it has no load/tree
confound — but it carries the spill-path confound at full strength, and its `CODE_REVIEW` binding
differs, so its turn structure is not this repo's. **Report per repo; never take a median across
repos.**

**What P2c reframes.** Bill/turn holds at CV 15% across all eight sessions while turn count spans
4.6×, and the reason is structural, not statistical: **average context is bounded above by the
compaction ceiling and below by the starting footprint, while turn count has no ceiling.** So
`cost ≈ turns × ~33k`, and convergence (Finding 2) is worth multiples of sharding. Sharding should
still ship — bounded, testable, closes a fail-open — but it is not the main cost lever.

### ⚠ P2's baseline shifts once v0.2.1 releases — do not double-count

**P2 above (53,693 → ~38,000) is stated against the *pre-#124* engine, and using it after v0.2.1
releases would credit the sharding refactor with a saving #124 already delivered.**

#124 removes the ~1.27× truncation-recovery multiplier on its own, taking P2 from **53,693 to
~46,000** with the file unchanged. The sharding refactor's contribution is what happens *after*
that. So:

| | P2 baseline | P2 predicted |
|---|---:|---:|
| #124 alone (v0.2.1) | 53,693 | **~46,000** |
| sharding, measured after v0.2.1 | **~46,000** | ~38,000 |

**Re-freeze this document's P2 row against ≥5 completed, non-live sessions on the installed v0.2.1
before judging the refactor**, and name the installed version on both sides of the comparison. P1,
P3 and P4 are unaffected: #124 moves none of them (it changes always-loaded by +267 tokens, in the
*opposite* direction, which is expected and is not a P1 failure).

**Falsifiers.** P1 is settled by `wc -c` and cannot fail without the change not being made. **P2
failing is the real risk**: if phase units get re-read after compaction, or the parent loads units it
does not need, engine load per session could *rise* even though always-loaded fell.

## Sequencing note — the measurement gap

**A change to `skills/` does not affect any run until a `plugin.json` bump and a re-install.** So the
after-measurement is separated from the before-measurement by a release, and sessions in between are
driven by the old engine. Record the installed version alongside every future measurement; a
before/after that does not name both engine versions is not interpretable.

Two changes are already queued ahead of the refactor and each shifts the baseline:

- **[PR #124](https://github.com/frederick-douglas-pearce/claude-code-loop/pull/124)** (F105) should
  move **P2 alone**, from ~53.7k toward ~46k, by removing the 1.27× recovery multiplier — with
  always-loaded unchanged. That is a clean, isolated prediction and a good rehearsal for the method.
- Anything that edits `loop-engine.md` changes P1 directly.

**Re-measure after each, not only at the end.** Two changes landing in one release make both
uninterpretable.

## Second per-repo baseline — `claude-code-loop` on 0.2.0

*(frozen 2026-08-26, before this repo upgrades to v0.2.1)*

The original baseline is entirely vote-repo. This repo is being upgraded to v0.2.1 as well, so a
second, independent before/after becomes available — and it is **a different workload**: vote is
Python data analysis, this repo is markdown-as-product. If P2's predicted drop holds across both,
the mechanism is confirmed rather than observed once.

All three sessions are genuine loop iterations (verified by `- Budget:` lines, pipeline status
markers, and subagent trace directories — not by filename or size).

| session | engine reads | **engine load (tok)** | all file reads (tok) | engine share | peak parent ctx |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `b40adacf` (08-24) | 18 | **55,034** | 98,382 | 56% | 336,352 |
| `fd687f48` (08-23) | 18 | **62,083** | 116,601 | 53% | 433,680 |
| `a587e8e4` (08-22) | 53 | **34,669** | 153,455 | 23% | 327,619 |
| **median** | | **55,034** | | **53%** | **336,352** |

**P2 baseline for this repo: 55,034** — consistent with vote's 53,693, which is the expected result
since engine load is a function of the engine and how it is read, not of the repo's deliverable.

**`a587e8e4` is the outlier and is kept deliberately.** Its engine *share* is 23% against the others'
53–56%, not because it read the engine less (53 reads, the most of the three) but because its total
read volume is far higher — 48 `- Budget:` lines say it re-read the ledger heavily. It is a real
iteration with an unusual read mix, not a mis-classified session, and excluding it would be
selecting the data to fit. **Engine share is the metric it distorts; P2 (engine load) is not
distorted, which is why the median is quoted from all three.**

**Prediction, same as vote's:** v0.2.1 should take P2 from **55,034 → ~46,000** here too, by removing
the truncated-`cat` recovery multiplier. Always-loaded moves +267 tokens the *other* way, which is
expected and is not a P1 failure.
